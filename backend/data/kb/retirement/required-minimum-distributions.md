# Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

Tax-deferred retirement accounts cannot grow untaxed forever. Required minimum distributions (RMDs) are the minimum amounts you must withdraw each year once you reach a certain age.

## Who must take them

RMDs apply to traditional IRAs, SEP and SIMPLE IRAs, and most workplace plans such as 401(k), 403(b) and 457(b) plans. Roth IRAs do not require withdrawals during the original owner's lifetime, and since 2024 Roth balances in workplace plans are also exempt.

## When they start

Under current law (the SECURE 2.0 Act), RMDs generally begin at age 73, rising to 75 for people who turn 74 after 2032. Your first RMD can be delayed until April 1 of the following year, but then you would take two in one year. If you are still working, some workplace plans let you delay RMDs from that plan until you retire.

## How the amount is calculated

The RMD for each account is generally the prior December 31 balance divided by a life expectancy factor from IRS tables. The percentage rises as you age. For multiple IRAs, you calculate each RMD but can withdraw the total from any of them; workplace plans usually must be handled separately.

## Penalty for missing one

Missing an RMD triggers an excise tax of 25% of the amount not withdrawn, reduced to 10% if corrected promptly.

## Planning considerations

RMDs are taxed as ordinary income and can push retirees into higher brackets or affect Medicare premiums. Some people convert to Roth accounts in lower-income years before RMDs begin, or use qualified charitable distributions from IRAs after age 70½. A tax professional can help with these decisions.
