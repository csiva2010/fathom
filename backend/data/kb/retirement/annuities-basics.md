# Annuities: The Basics

An annuity is a contract with an insurance company. You pay a lump sum or a series of payments, and in return the insurer promises payments to you, either right away or in the future. Annuities range from simple to very complex.

## Main types

- **Immediate income annuity**: you hand over a lump sum and receive guaranteed payments for life or a set period, starting right away. It works like buying a personal pension and protects against outliving your money.
- **Fixed annuity**: pays a guaranteed interest rate for a set period, similar to a CD issued by an insurer.
- **Variable annuity**: your money is invested in sub-accounts similar to mutual funds, so the value rises and falls. Optional riders can add guarantees for an extra cost.
- **Indexed annuity**: returns are linked to a market index with a floor and a cap, using formulas that can be hard to understand.

## Costs and restrictions

Variable and indexed annuities often carry high annual fees, rider charges and surrender charges that can last many years if you withdraw early. Commissions to the seller can be substantial, which is why regulators publish investor alerts urging careful review.

## Taxes

Growth inside an annuity is tax-deferred, but withdrawals of earnings are taxed as ordinary income, and withdrawals before 59½ may face a 10% additional tax.

## Guarantees depend on the insurer

An annuity's guarantees are only as strong as the insurance company backing them. State guaranty associations provide limited protection if an insurer fails.

## Questions commonly asked

What problem does this solve, what are the total fees, how long is the surrender period, and is there a simpler, cheaper alternative?
