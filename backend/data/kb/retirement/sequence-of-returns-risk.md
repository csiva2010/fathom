# Sequence of Returns Risk

Sequence of returns risk is the danger that poor investment returns early in retirement, while you are withdrawing money, do lasting damage to a portfolio, even if average returns over the whole retirement turn out fine.

## Why order matters

When you are only saving, the order of good and bad years does not change your final balance for a given set of returns. When you are withdrawing, it does. Selling investments to cover spending after a market drop locks in losses and leaves fewer shares to benefit from the recovery.

Two retirees can experience the same average return over 30 years but end with very different outcomes if one faces a crash in year one and the other in year twenty-five.

## The risky window

The years just before and after retirement are often called the retirement red zone, because the portfolio is at or near its largest and withdrawals are about to start.

## Common ways people address it

- **Holding more bonds and cash near retirement**, so spending can come from stable assets while stocks recover.
- **A cash or bond bucket** covering a few years of spending.
- **Flexible withdrawals**: spending less after bad market years.
- **Guaranteed income**: delaying Social Security or buying an income annuity covers essential spending regardless of markets.
- **Working part time** or delaying retirement if a downturn arrives at the wrong moment.

## Key idea

Retirement planning is about not just average returns but surviving the bad sequences. Testing a plan against poor early returns gives a more realistic picture than a single average-return projection.
