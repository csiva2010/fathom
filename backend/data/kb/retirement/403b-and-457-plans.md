# 403(b) and 457(b) Plans

Not every employer offers a 401(k). Public schools, hospitals, nonprofits and government employers often offer 403(b) or 457(b) plans, which work in similar ways with a few important differences.

## 403(b) plans

A 403(b) is offered by public schools, colleges and many tax-exempt organizations. Like a 401(k), you contribute from your paycheck, traditional contributions lower taxable income now, and many plans offer a Roth option. Contribution limits generally match the 401(k) limit. Some long-serving employees may qualify for an extra catch-up amount.

Historically, many 403(b) plans offered annuity products with high fees. Checking the expense ratios of available options is worthwhile.

## 457(b) plans

A 457(b) is offered mainly by state and local governments. Its distinctive feature: for governmental 457(b) plans, withdrawals after you leave the employer are not subject to the 10% early withdrawal tax, even before age 59½. Taxes on traditional contributions still apply.

Because the 457(b) limit is separate from the 401(k)/403(b) limit, someone eligible for both a 403(b) and a 457(b) may be able to contribute the full amount to each.

## Similarities with 401(k)s

- Annual limits set by the IRS, with catch-up contributions from age 50.
- Employer contributions may vest over time.
- Required minimum distributions apply to traditional balances starting at the required age.
- Rollovers to IRAs or other plans are generally allowed when you leave.

## Check your plan documents

Rules differ by plan. The summary plan description explains contribution options, matching, vesting and withdrawal rules.
