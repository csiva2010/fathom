# Retirement Plans for the Self-Employed

If you are self-employed or run a small business, you do not have an employer's 401(k), but you can open your own retirement plan. The main options differ in how much you can save and how much paperwork they involve.

## SEP IRA

A Simplified Employee Pension lets the business contribute a percentage of each eligible person's compensation, up to an annual limit. It is easy to set up and maintain, with no annual filing in most cases. Contributions are made by the employer only, and if you have eligible employees, you must contribute the same percentage for them.

## Solo 401(k)

A one-participant 401(k) is for business owners with no employees other than a spouse. You contribute both as the "employee" (up to the regular 401(k) deferral limit, including any catch-up) and as the "employer" (a percentage of compensation). This combination often allows the highest contributions at moderate incomes. Many providers offer a Roth option. Once the plan's assets exceed a threshold, an annual Form 5500-EZ filing is required.

## SIMPLE IRA

A Savings Incentive Match Plan for Employees suits small businesses with employees. Employees can defer part of their pay, and the employer must either match up to a set percentage or make a fixed contribution for everyone. Limits are lower than a 401(k).

## Plus an IRA

Self-employed people can usually still contribute to a traditional or Roth IRA, subject to the usual rules.

## Choosing

Common factors are expected income, whether you have employees, how much you want to save, and how much administration you are willing to handle. A tax professional can confirm the best fit.
