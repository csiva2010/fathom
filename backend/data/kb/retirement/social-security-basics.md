# Social Security Retirement Benefits

Social Security provides a monthly retirement benefit to workers who have paid Social Security taxes, and for most retirees it is an important income source that lasts for life and rises with inflation.

## Earning eligibility

You earn credits by working and paying Social Security taxes. Most people need 40 credits, roughly 10 years of work, to qualify for retirement benefits. Your benefit is based on your highest 35 years of inflation-adjusted earnings.

## When you can claim

- **Age 62**: the earliest age to claim, with a permanently reduced benefit.
- **Full retirement age (FRA)**: 67 for people born in 1960 or later. You receive your full benefit.
- **Age 70**: benefits grow for each year you delay past FRA, by 8% a year, up to age 70. There is no extra benefit for waiting beyond 70.

Claiming at 62 versus 70 can mean a monthly benefit that differs by roughly 75% or more for the rest of your life.

## Things that affect the decision

People weigh health and life expectancy, other income and savings, whether they are still working, and spousal benefits. For married couples, the higher earner delaying can increase the survivor benefit the other spouse may receive.

## Inflation adjustments and taxes

Benefits receive cost-of-living adjustments based on inflation. Depending on your other income, part of your benefit may be subject to federal income tax.

## Check your record

A my Social Security account at ssa.gov shows your earnings history and estimated benefits at different claiming ages. Errors in earnings records are worth correcting early.
