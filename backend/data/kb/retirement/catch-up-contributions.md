# Catch-Up Contributions and Saving After 50

Once you turn 50, the IRS lets you contribute more than the standard annual limit to many retirement accounts. These extra amounts are called catch-up contributions and are meant to help people who are behind on saving.

## Which accounts allow catch-ups

- **401(k), 403(b) and governmental 457(b) plans**: an extra catch-up amount on top of the regular limit.
- **IRAs**: an extra catch-up amount on top of the regular IRA limit, now indexed to inflation.
- **SIMPLE IRAs**: their own, smaller catch-up amount.
- **HSAs**: a separate catch-up from age 55.

The dollar amounts change most years; the IRS publishes them each fall.

## Recent changes under SECURE 2.0

- A higher catch-up limit applies in workplace plans for people aged 60 through 63.
- Higher earners (above an income threshold set by law) must make workplace catch-up contributions as Roth contributions.

## Why it can matter

Your fifties are often peak earning years, and children may be grown. Extra contributions, plus a decade or more of growth, can make a meaningful difference by retirement. Traditional catch-ups also reduce taxable income in what may be high-bracket years.

## Other levers after 50

People in this stage often also review their asset allocation for the approaching withdrawal years, estimate Social Security at different claiming ages, plan for health insurance before Medicare at 65, and pay down debt before retiring.
