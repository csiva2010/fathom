# Building an Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected expenses or a loss of income, such as a car repair, a medical bill or time between jobs. It is usually the first financial goal educators suggest, before investing.

## Why it comes first

Without a cushion, a surprise expense often lands on a credit card at a high interest rate, or forces you to sell investments at a bad time. An emergency fund protects both your budget and your long-term investments, which is why it is sometimes described as the foundation of a financial plan.

## How much to save

A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses: rent or mortgage, utilities, food, insurance, transportation and minimum debt payments. People with variable income, a single income in the household, or less job security often aim for the higher end. If that target feels large, a starter goal such as $1,000 can cover many smaller emergencies while you keep building.

## Where to keep it

The money should be safe and easy to reach, not invested in the stock market where it could drop just when you need it. Common choices are high-yield savings accounts and money market accounts at insured institutions. Keeping it in a separate account from everyday spending reduces the temptation to use it for non-emergencies.

## How to build it

Treat it like a bill: set up an automatic transfer on payday, even a small one. Direct windfalls such as tax refunds or bonuses toward the fund. Track your progress; seeing the balance grow helps keep the habit going.

## Using and refilling it

Use it only for true emergencies, then rebuild it as soon as you can. Planned costs that happen every year, such as holidays or insurance premiums, are better handled with separate savings goals.
