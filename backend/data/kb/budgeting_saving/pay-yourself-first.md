# Pay Yourself First and Automate Saving

"Pay yourself first" means treating savings like a bill that gets paid before anything else, rather than saving whatever happens to be left at the end of the month.

## Why it works

Spending tends to expand to fill whatever money is available. Moving savings out of your checking account as soon as you are paid removes the temptation and the need for willpower each month. It turns saving into a default rather than a decision.

## How to automate it

- **Payroll deductions**: workplace retirement contributions come out before your paycheck arrives.
- **Split direct deposit**: many employers can send part of each paycheck straight to a savings account.
- **Scheduled transfers**: set an automatic transfer from checking to savings or a brokerage account on payday.
- **Automatic investments**: many brokers and IRAs allow recurring purchases of funds.

## Start small and step up

If saving 15% feels impossible, start with 1% or 2% and raise it gradually, for example each time you get a raise. Some 401(k) plans offer automatic escalation, which increases your contribution rate by a point each year.

## Keep a buffer

Automating too aggressively can lead to overdrafts. Keeping a small cushion in checking, or timing transfers just after payday, helps avoid fees.

## The bigger idea

Behavioral research consistently finds that defaults have a powerful effect on saving. Automatic enrollment in workplace plans dramatically increases participation. Setting up good defaults once can do more than repeated good intentions.
