# Saving for a Home Down Payment

Buying a home usually requires a down payment plus cash for closing costs and moving. Planning ahead makes the goal concrete and helps decide where to keep the money.

## How much is needed?

A 20% down payment avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI) on a conventional loan, but many buyers put down less. Some loans require 3% to 5%, and FHA loans as little as 3.5% for qualified borrowers. Smaller down payments mean larger loans, higher monthly payments and usually mortgage insurance.

Do not forget closing costs, often around 2% to 5% of the loan amount, plus moving costs and a cushion for repairs.

## Setting the goal

Estimate a realistic price range, multiply by your target down payment percentage, add closing costs, and divide by the months until you hope to buy. That gives a monthly savings target. This assistant's goal planner can project progress and the required monthly amount.

## Where to keep the money

Because the timeline is often a few years and the amount must be there when you need it, many people keep down payment savings in high-yield savings accounts, CDs, Treasury bills or money market funds rather than stocks, which can fall sharply right before a purchase.

## Other considerations

- First-time buyer assistance programs offered by states and localities.
- Your credit score, which affects your mortgage rate.
- Keeping a separate emergency fund so homeownership surprises do not become debt.
- The total monthly cost, including taxes, insurance and maintenance, not just the mortgage payment.
