# Budgeting Basics and the 50/30/20 Rule

A budget is a plan for your money: how much comes in, where it goes, and what is left for saving. It is the foundation for every other financial goal, from paying down debt to investing.

## Start by tracking

Before setting limits, look at what you actually spend over a month or two. Bank and card statements or budgeting apps make this easier. Many people are surprised by small recurring costs such as subscriptions and delivery fees.

## The 50/30/20 rule

A popular starting framework splits after-tax income into three buckets:

- **50% for needs**: housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, transportation, minimum debt payments.
- **30% for wants**: dining out, entertainment, travel, hobbies.
- **20% for savings and extra debt payments**: emergency fund, retirement, other goals.

It is a guideline, not a rule. In high-cost areas, needs may take more than half of income; someone aggressively paying off debt might push the savings bucket well above 20%.

## Other approaches

- **Zero-based budgeting**: every dollar is assigned a job until income minus planned spending equals zero.
- **Envelope method**: set amounts of cash, or digital envelopes, for spending categories.
- **Pay-yourself-first**: automate savings on payday and spend what remains.

## Making it stick

Review your budget monthly, adjust for irregular expenses, and leave some room for fun so the plan is sustainable. The best budget is one you will actually follow.
