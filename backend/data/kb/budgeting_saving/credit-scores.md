# How Credit Scores Work

A credit score is a three-digit number that estimates how likely you are to repay borrowed money. Lenders use it to decide whether to approve loans and credit cards and what interest rate to charge. Landlords, insurers and some employers may also check credit information.

## What goes into a score

The widely used FICO score ranges from 300 to 850. Its main factors, in rough order of importance:

- **Payment history**: whether you pay on time. Late payments hurt the most.
- **Amounts owed**: especially credit utilization, the share of available revolving credit you are using. Lower is better; many guides suggest staying under 30%, and lower still for the best scores.
- **Length of credit history**: older accounts help.
- **New credit**: many recent applications can lower a score temporarily.
- **Credit mix**: a combination of revolving and installment credit can help slightly.

VantageScore uses similar factors.

## Credit reports

Scores are calculated from your credit reports at the three nationwide bureaus: Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. You can get free reports from each at AnnualCreditReport.com and dispute errors you find.

## Building or rebuilding credit

- Pay every bill on time; autopay helps.
- Keep card balances low relative to limits.
- Keep older accounts open when practical.
- Secured cards and credit-builder loans can help people with thin or damaged credit.

## Why it matters

A higher score can mean meaningfully lower interest rates on mortgages and auto loans, which can save thousands of dollars over the life of a loan.
