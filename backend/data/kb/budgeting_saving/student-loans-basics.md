# Student Loan Basics

Student loans help pay for education, but they are a long-term commitment. Understanding the types and repayment options makes them easier to manage.

## Federal versus private loans

- **Federal student loans** are issued by the US Department of Education. They have fixed interest rates set by law and borrower protections such as deferment, forbearance, income-driven repayment and certain forgiveness programs.
- **Private student loans** come from banks, credit unions and online lenders. Rates and terms depend on credit and are often variable. They usually have fewer protections.

Federal loans are generally tapped first for this reason.

## Subsidized and unsubsidized

For subsidized federal loans, available to undergraduates with financial need, the government pays the interest while you are in school at least half time. For unsubsidized loans, interest builds up from the start and is added to the balance later.

## Repayment options

Federal loans offer standard fixed plans, graduated and extended plans, and income-driven plans that tie payments to income and family size. Repayment rules have changed several times in recent years, so checking StudentAid.gov for the current plans is important.

## Forgiveness programs

Public Service Loan Forgiveness can forgive remaining federal Direct Loan balances after qualifying payments while working full time for government or eligible nonprofits. Program rules are strict, so tracking certification matters.

## Paying down versus investing

Borrowers often weigh extra loan payments against investing. The interest rate, the loan type and access to forgiveness all factor in, and capturing any employer retirement match is commonly prioritized either way.
