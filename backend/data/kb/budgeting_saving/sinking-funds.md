# Sinking Funds for Irregular Expenses

A sinking fund is money you set aside gradually for a specific expense you know is coming, so it does not catch your budget off guard.

## How it differs from an emergency fund

An emergency fund covers the unexpected: a job loss, a medical bill, a sudden repair. A sinking fund covers the expected but irregular: annual insurance premiums, holiday gifts, car registration, a new laptop, a vacation. Separating the two keeps your emergency fund intact for real emergencies.

## How to set one up

1. List predictable expenses that do not happen monthly.
2. Estimate each cost and when it is due.
3. Divide the cost by the months remaining. A $1,200 insurance bill due in 12 months needs $100 a month.
4. Automate monthly transfers into a separate savings account, or into labeled buckets that some banks offer within one account.

## Common sinking fund categories

- Car maintenance and replacement.
- Home repairs and maintenance.
- Annual subscriptions and memberships.
- Medical deductibles.
- Gifts and holidays.
- Travel.
- Pet care.

## Why it helps

Irregular expenses are a major reason people end up using credit cards and carrying balances. Spreading those costs evenly across the year makes your monthly budget more realistic and reduces stress when the bills arrive.
