# High-Yield Savings Accounts

A high-yield savings account is a savings account that pays a noticeably higher interest rate than the national average. They are most often offered by online banks and credit unions with lower overhead costs.

## APY

Rates are quoted as annual percentage yield (APY), which includes the effect of compounding during the year. Comparing APYs is the simplest way to compare accounts. Rates are variable: they can change at any time and tend to follow the Federal Reserve's interest rate moves.

## Safety

At FDIC-insured banks, deposits are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. Credit unions have equivalent NCUA insurance. Checking that an institution is insured is an important step, especially with fintech apps that partner with banks behind the scenes.

## What they are good for

- Emergency funds.
- Short-term goals such as a vacation or car purchase.
- Sinking funds for irregular expenses.
- Money waiting to be invested.

## Things to check

- Minimum balance requirements and monthly fees.
- Limits on the number of withdrawals per month, which some banks still apply.
- How quickly transfers reach your checking account; online banks can take one to three business days.
- Introductory rates that drop after a few months.

## Limits

Even a high savings rate may only roughly match inflation over time. Savings accounts are for stability and access, not long-term growth.
