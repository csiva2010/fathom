# How Credit Cards Work

Credit cards let you borrow up to a set limit and repay over time. Used carefully, they offer convenience, purchase protection and rewards; used carelessly, they can become one of the most expensive kinds of debt.

## The grace period

Most cards give a grace period between the end of a billing cycle and the payment due date. If you pay the full statement balance by the due date every month, you usually pay no interest on purchases. Carrying any balance typically means interest on that balance and often on new purchases too.

## APR and how interest adds up

Card interest is quoted as an annual percentage rate (APR) but is usually calculated daily on your balance. With APRs often above 20%, a carried balance grows quickly.

## The minimum payment trap

The minimum payment is often a small percentage of the balance. Paying only the minimum can stretch repayment out for many years and multiply the total interest paid. Statements are required to show how long payoff would take at the minimum.

## Fees to watch

Annual fees, late fees, cash advance fees (with higher APRs and no grace period), balance transfer fees and foreign transaction fees.

## Credit score effects

Payment history and credit utilization, the share of your limit you use, are major credit score factors. Paying on time and keeping balances low help your score.

## Rewards in perspective

Rewards are only worth it if you pay in full. A typical 1% to 2% back is easily wiped out by interest at 20% or more.
