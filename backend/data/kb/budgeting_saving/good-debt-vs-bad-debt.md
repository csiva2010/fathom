# Interest Rates and Types of Debt

Not all debt is the same. The interest rate, what the money pays for, and the repayment terms all determine whether a debt helps or hurts your finances.

## Debt that can build value

Some borrowing finances things that may increase your net worth or earning power over time:

- **Mortgages** help buy a home that may appreciate, usually at relatively low rates over long terms.
- **Student loans** can fund education that raises lifetime earnings, although the payoff depends on the field and amount borrowed.

Even "good" debt can become a problem if the payment is too large for your budget.

## Costly debt

- **Credit card balances** carried month to month often charge high APRs, frequently over 20%.
- **Payday loans and title loans** can carry triple-digit annual rates and can trap borrowers in repeated borrowing.
- **Buy now, pay later** plans can be interest-free if paid on time but make overspending easy and can charge late fees.

## Comparing debt with investing

A useful way to think about it: paying off a debt is like earning a guaranteed return equal to its interest rate. Paying off a 22% credit card is a far better "return" than investments are likely to provide, which is why high-interest debt is often prioritized. Low-rate debt, such as a 3% mortgage, is a closer call, and people weigh investing extra money against prepaying.

## Understanding APR

The annual percentage rate (APR) includes interest and certain fees, making it easier to compare loans. Credit cards may have different APRs for purchases, balance transfers and cash advances.
