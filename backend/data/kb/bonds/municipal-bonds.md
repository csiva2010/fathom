# Municipal Bonds

Municipal bonds, or munis, are issued by states, cities, counties and other public entities to fund projects such as schools, roads and water systems. Their main attraction is that interest is usually exempt from federal income tax.

## Types of munis

- **General obligation bonds** are backed by the issuer's taxing power.
- **Revenue bonds** are repaid from a specific project's income, such as tolls, airport fees or utility payments, and depend on that project's success.

## Tax advantages

Interest from most munis is free from federal income tax. If you buy bonds issued in your own state, interest is often free from state income tax as well. Some munis are subject to the alternative minimum tax, and capital gains from selling munis are taxable.

## Tax-equivalent yield

Because of the tax break, munis pay lower stated yields than taxable bonds. To compare them, investors calculate the tax-equivalent yield: the muni yield divided by (1 minus your tax rate). For someone in a 32% bracket, a 3% muni yield is equivalent to about 4.4% from a taxable bond. Munis are generally more attractive to people in higher brackets.

## Risks

- **Credit risk**: municipal defaults have been rare historically but do happen, especially for some revenue bonds.
- **Interest rate risk**: prices fall when rates rise.
- **Liquidity**: many individual munis trade infrequently, making them harder to sell at a fair price. Muni funds offer more liquidity and diversification.

Munis rarely make sense inside IRAs or 401(k)s, since those accounts are already tax-advantaged.
