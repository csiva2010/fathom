# Series I and EE Savings Bonds

US savings bonds are low-risk government bonds sold directly to individuals through TreasuryDirect. Unlike Treasury bills or notes, they cannot be traded on the market, and their value never falls.

## Series I bonds

I bonds earn a combined rate made of two parts: a fixed rate set when you buy, which stays the same for the bond's life, and an inflation rate that resets every six months based on the CPI. When inflation is high, I bond rates rise with it. The combined rate cannot drop below zero.

## Series EE bonds

EE bonds earn a fixed rate set at purchase. The Treasury guarantees that an EE bond will at least double in value if held for 20 years, which can make its effective return higher than the stated rate for people who hold them that long.

## Key rules for both

- You must hold a savings bond for at least 12 months before cashing it.
- If you cash it before five years, you give up the last three months of interest.
- Bonds earn interest for up to 30 years.
- There is an annual purchase limit per person for electronic bonds bought through TreasuryDirect; check TreasuryDirect for current limits.

## Taxes

Interest is subject to federal income tax but not state or local income tax. You can defer federal tax until you cash the bond or it matures. Interest may be tax-free if used for qualified higher education expenses and income limits are met.

## Where they fit

Savings bonds are used by some people for part of an emergency fund (after the first year), inflation protection, or simple long-term saving. Their main drawbacks are purchase limits and the one-year lockup.
