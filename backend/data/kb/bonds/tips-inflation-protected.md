# Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS)

Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) are US government bonds designed to protect investors from inflation. Their principal value rises with inflation and falls with deflation, as measured by the Consumer Price Index.

## How TIPS work

TIPS pay a fixed interest rate twice a year, but the rate is applied to the inflation-adjusted principal. If inflation rises, the principal grows, so the interest payments grow too. At maturity, you receive the adjusted principal or the original principal, whichever is greater, so deflation cannot push you below what you started with at maturity.

TIPS are issued with 5, 10 and 30-year maturities.

## Real yield

The quoted yield on TIPS is a real yield, meaning a return above inflation. Comparing a TIPS yield with a regular Treasury yield of the same maturity gives the breakeven inflation rate: the inflation level at which both would earn the same.

## Risks and quirks

- **Interest rate risk**: TIPS prices still fall when real interest rates rise. Long-term TIPS can lose significant value before maturity.
- **Tax on phantom income**: in taxable accounts, the yearly inflation adjustment to principal is taxable even though you do not receive it until maturity. Many investors hold TIPS in tax-advantaged accounts for this reason.
- **Measured inflation**: adjustments follow CPI, which may differ from your personal inflation rate.

## Ways to own them

You can buy TIPS through TreasuryDirect, through a broker, or via TIPS mutual funds and ETFs. Funds offer convenience but, unlike individual TIPS held to maturity, do not return a guaranteed principal amount.
