# US Treasury Bills, Notes and Bonds

The US Treasury borrows money by selling securities to investors. Because they are backed by the full faith and credit of the US government, Treasuries are considered to have very low credit risk and serve as a benchmark for other interest rates.

## The main types

- **Treasury bills (T-bills)**: short-term securities maturing in up to one year. They are sold at a discount and pay face value at maturity; the difference is your interest.
- **Treasury notes**: maturities of 2, 3, 5, 7 or 10 years, paying interest every six months.
- **Treasury bonds**: maturities of 20 or 30 years, also paying interest every six months.
- **TIPS**: Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities, whose principal adjusts with inflation.
- **Floating rate notes**: two-year securities whose interest resets with T-bill rates.

## How to buy them

You can buy Treasuries directly at auction through TreasuryDirect, through a brokerage account at auction or on the secondary market, or indirectly through Treasury mutual funds and ETFs.

## Tax treatment

Interest on Treasuries is subject to federal income tax but exempt from state and local income taxes, which can matter for residents of high-tax states.

## Risks that remain

Treasuries are not risk-free in every sense. Their market prices fall when interest rates rise, especially for long maturities, so selling before maturity can mean a loss. And inflation can erode the purchasing power of fixed payments, which TIPS are designed to address.
