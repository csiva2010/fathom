# Asset Location: Which Investments Go in Which Account

Asset allocation decides what mix of investments you hold. Asset location decides which accounts hold them. Placing each investment in the account where it is taxed least can raise after-tax returns without changing risk.

## The three account types

- **Taxable** brokerage accounts: income and realized gains are taxed each year.
- **Tax-deferred** accounts (traditional IRA, 401(k)): no tax until withdrawal, then ordinary income rates.
- **Tax-free** accounts (Roth IRA, Roth 401(k)): qualified withdrawals are tax-free.

## General tendencies

**Tax-inefficient** holdings produce a lot of income taxed at ordinary rates: taxable bonds, REITs, high-turnover active funds. They are often placed in tax-advantaged accounts.

**Tax-efficient** holdings, such as broad stock index funds and ETFs, produce mostly qualified dividends and few capital gains distributions. They suffer least in taxable accounts and also benefit from lower long-term capital gains rates, the step-up in basis at death, and the ability to harvest losses.

**Highest expected growth** is often placed in Roth accounts, because all of that growth comes out tax-free.

## Caveats

- Municipal bonds belong in taxable accounts if anywhere, since their interest is already federally tax-exempt.
- Asset location only applies if you have more than one account type with room to choose.
- Rebalancing gets more complex when each asset class lives in a different account.
- Benefits are real but usually modest; keeping the overall allocation right matters more.
