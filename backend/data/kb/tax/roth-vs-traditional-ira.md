# Roth IRA vs Traditional IRA

An individual retirement account (IRA) is a tax-advantaged account you open yourself, separate from any employer plan. The two main types differ in when you pay tax.

## Traditional IRA: tax break now

Contributions to a traditional IRA may be tax-deductible, which lowers your taxable income in the year you contribute. The money grows tax-deferred, and withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income. Whether your contribution is deductible depends on your income and whether you or your spouse are covered by a workplace retirement plan.

## Roth IRA: tax break later

Contributions to a Roth IRA are made with money you have already paid tax on, so there is no deduction today. In return, qualified withdrawals in retirement, including all the growth, are tax-free. You can generally withdraw your original contributions (not earnings) at any time without tax or penalty. Eligibility to contribute directly to a Roth IRA phases out above certain income levels.

## How people think about the choice

The core question is whether your tax rate is likely to be higher now or in retirement. If you expect a higher rate later, paying tax now with a Roth can be attractive. If you are in a high bracket today and expect a lower one in retirement, the traditional deduction may be worth more. Because the future is uncertain, some people split contributions between both to diversify their tax treatment.

## Shared rules

Both types share one annual contribution limit, which the IRS adjusts periodically, plus a catch-up amount from age 50. You need earned income to contribute. Earnings withdrawn before age 59½ are generally subject to tax and a 10% additional tax unless an exception applies. Traditional IRAs require minimum distributions starting at a set age; Roth IRAs do not require them during the original owner's lifetime.

## A note on this information

Tax rules and limits change. This article explains concepts only; for your specific situation, check current IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional.
