# Tax Brackets: Marginal vs Effective Rates

The US federal income tax is progressive: higher slices of income are taxed at higher rates. Understanding how brackets work clears up one of the most common money myths.

## How brackets work

Income is divided into bands, each taxed at its own rate, currently ranging from 10% to 37%. Only the income within each band is taxed at that band's rate. Moving into a higher bracket does not raise the tax on the income you already had; it only affects the dollars above the threshold.

So a raise can never reduce your take-home pay through the bracket system alone.

## Marginal rate

Your marginal tax rate is the rate on your next dollar of income, the top bracket your income reaches. It is the right number for decisions such as whether a traditional (pre-tax) retirement contribution saves more tax than a Roth contribution, because a deduction removes dollars from your top bracket.

## Effective rate

Your effective tax rate is your total tax divided by your total income. Because lower slices are taxed at lower rates, and because of the standard deduction, the effective rate is always below the marginal rate.

## Taxable income

Brackets apply to taxable income, which is your income after subtracting the standard deduction (or itemized deductions) and certain adjustments, such as traditional 401(k) contributions. Long-term capital gains and qualified dividends use their own separate rate schedule.

## Where to find current numbers

Bracket thresholds are adjusted for inflation each year. The IRS publishes the current tables, and they differ by filing status.
