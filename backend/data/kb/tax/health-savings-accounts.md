# Health Savings Accounts (HSAs)

A health savings account (HSA) is a tax-advantaged account for medical expenses, available to people covered by a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP). It is often described as the only account with a triple tax advantage.

## The triple tax advantage

1. **Contributions** are tax-deductible, or pre-tax if made through payroll, which can also avoid payroll taxes.
2. **Growth** is tax-free while the money stays in the account.
3. **Withdrawals** for qualified medical expenses are tax-free.

## Eligibility and limits

You must be covered by an HSA-eligible high-deductible plan and have no disqualifying coverage, such as Medicare. The IRS sets annual contribution limits for self-only and family coverage, plus a catch-up amount from age 55. Employer contributions count toward the limit.

## It is your account

Unlike a flexible spending account (FSA), HSA money does not have to be spent by year-end. The balance rolls over indefinitely and stays with you if you change jobs. Many HSAs let you invest the balance in funds once it passes a minimum.

## Withdrawals for other purposes

Before age 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed as income plus a 20% additional tax. After 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income without the penalty, much like a traditional IRA.

## A retirement strategy some use

Some people pay current medical costs out of pocket, invest the HSA for decades, keep receipts, and reimburse themselves tax-free years later. Health care is a major expense in retirement, so an invested HSA can be a powerful supplement to retirement savings.
