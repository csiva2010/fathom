# 529 Education Savings Plans

A 529 plan is a tax-advantaged account for education savings, named after the section of the tax code that created it. Plans are sponsored by states, but you can usually use any state's plan.

## Tax benefits

- Earnings grow free of federal income tax.
- Withdrawals for qualified education expenses are tax-free.
- Many states offer a state income tax deduction or credit for contributions, often only for their own plan.

## Qualified expenses

Tuition, fees, books, supplies and equipment at eligible colleges, universities and trade schools, plus room and board for students enrolled at least half time. Up to a set annual amount can be used for K-12 tuition, and limited amounts can go toward registered apprenticeships and student loan repayment.

## If the money is not used for education

The earnings portion of a non-qualified withdrawal is subject to income tax plus a 10% additional tax. Options to avoid that include changing the beneficiary to another family member, or, under SECURE 2.0, rolling up to a lifetime maximum of $35,000 into a Roth IRA for the beneficiary, if the account has been open at least 15 years and other rules are met.

## Investment choices

Most plans offer age-based portfolios that become more conservative as the student nears college, along with static stock, bond and cash options. Fees vary considerably between plans.

## Financial aid

A parent-owned 529 is treated as a parental asset for federal aid, which generally has a modest effect on aid eligibility.
