# How Dividends and Interest Are Taxed

In a taxable account, income from investments is taxed in the year you receive it, even if it is automatically reinvested. Different kinds of income are taxed differently.

## Qualified dividends

Dividends from most US companies and many foreign companies are qualified if you meet a holding-period rule: generally, owning the shares for more than 60 days during the 121-day period around the ex-dividend date. Qualified dividends are taxed at the long-term capital gains rates of 0%, 15% or 20%.

## Ordinary (non-qualified) dividends

Dividends that do not meet the rules are taxed at your ordinary income rate. Common examples include most REIT distributions, dividends from money market funds, and dividends on shares held only briefly.

## Interest income

Interest from savings accounts, CDs, corporate bonds and bond funds is taxed as ordinary income. Treasury interest is exempt from state and local income tax. Most municipal bond interest is exempt from federal income tax.

## Fund distributions

Mutual funds and ETFs pass through dividends and interest, and may also distribute capital gains when they sell holdings at a profit. Capital gains distributions are taxed as long-term gains regardless of how long you have owned the fund. ETFs and index funds tend to distribute fewer capital gains.

## Forms

Brokers send Form 1099-DIV for dividends and capital gains distributions and Form 1099-INT for interest.

## Inside retirement accounts

Dividends and interest earned in IRAs, 401(k)s and HSAs are not taxed when paid. This is one reason people place tax-inefficient investments in tax-advantaged accounts, a strategy called asset location.
