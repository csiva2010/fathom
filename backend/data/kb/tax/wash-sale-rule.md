# The Wash Sale Rule

The wash sale rule stops investors from selling an investment at a loss just to claim a tax deduction while effectively keeping the same position.

## The rule

A wash sale happens when you sell a security at a loss and, within 30 days before or after the sale, you buy a "substantially identical" security. The window covers 61 days in total. When it applies, you cannot deduct the loss at that time.

## What happens to the loss

The disallowed loss is not lost forever. It is added to the cost basis of the replacement shares, and the holding period of the old shares carries over. You recognize the loss later when you sell the replacement shares, as long as that sale is not itself a wash sale.

## What counts as substantially identical

The IRS has not given a precise definition. Shares of the same company and options on them are covered. Two funds tracking the same index are a gray area. Many investors swap into a fund that tracks a different but similar index to stay invested while avoiding the rule.

## Easy ways to trigger it accidentally

- Automatic dividend reinvestment in the same fund within the window.
- Buying the same security in an IRA or another account, including a spouse's account. Purchases in an IRA can permanently disallow the loss, since the basis adjustment does not carry into the IRA.

## Scope

The rule applies to losses, not gains. Brokers track wash sales within the same account on Form 1099-B, but not across different accounts or institutions, so investors must track those themselves.
