# Tax-Loss Harvesting

Tax-loss harvesting means selling investments in a taxable account that have fallen below their purchase price, to realize a capital loss that can reduce your taxes, while keeping your overall investment plan intact.

## How it works

1. An investment in your taxable account falls below its cost basis.
2. You sell it, realizing the loss.
3. You immediately buy a similar but not substantially identical investment, so your market exposure stays about the same.
4. The realized loss offsets capital gains, and up to $3,000 a year of ordinary income, with any excess carried forward indefinitely.

## Why it helps

The tax savings can be reinvested. Harvesting does lower your cost basis, so you may pay more tax later when you sell, but deferring tax has value, and the later gain may be taxed at lower long-term rates or never if the shares are held until death or donated.

## Watch the wash sale rule

Buying a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale disallows the loss. That includes purchases in IRAs and automatic dividend reinvestments.

## When it does not apply

- Losses inside IRAs, 401(k)s and other tax-advantaged accounts cannot be harvested.
- People in the 0% long-term capital gains bracket may benefit more from harvesting gains than losses.

## Practical notes

Many robo-advisers automate harvesting. Doing it manually works best with broad index funds where a close substitute exists. Record keeping matters, especially across multiple accounts.
