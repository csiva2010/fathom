# How to Read a Stock Quote

A stock quote is a snapshot of a stock's trading information. Knowing the common fields makes market data much easier to understand.

## Common fields

- **Ticker symbol**: a short code for the security, such as AAPL or SPY.
- **Last price**: the price of the most recent trade.
- **Change and percent change**: how far the price has moved from the previous day's closing price.
- **Bid and ask**: the highest price buyers are currently offering and the lowest price sellers will accept. The gap between them is the spread.
- **Open, high and low**: the first trade price of the day and the day's highest and lowest prices.
- **Previous close**: the last price of the prior regular trading session.
- **Volume**: how many shares have traded today. Unusually high volume often accompanies news.
- **52-week range**: the lowest and highest prices over the past year.
- **Market cap**: share price times shares outstanding.
- **P/E ratio and dividend yield**: valuation and income measures.

## Real-time, delayed and cached data

Quotes can be real-time or delayed (often by 15 minutes on free services). Outside market hours, the "last price" is usually the previous close or an after-hours trade. Good tools label where data comes from and how fresh it is, as this assistant does with its freshness badges.

## What a quote does not tell you

A quote shows what the market is paying right now. It does not tell you whether a company is well run or whether a price is "right." Daily moves are often noise; long-term results depend on the business and on broader market conditions.
