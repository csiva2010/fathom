# International Stocks

International stocks are shares of companies based outside the United States. US companies make up a large share of the world's stock market value, but not all of it, so many diversified portfolios include international holdings.

## Developed and emerging markets

- **Developed markets** include countries such as Japan, the United Kingdom, Canada, Germany, France and Australia, with mature economies and established markets.
- **Emerging markets** include countries such as China, India, Brazil and Taiwan, with faster-growing but often less stable economies and markets.

Broad international index funds hold thousands of companies across these regions.

## Why investors hold them

US and international markets have taken turns leading over long periods. Holding both spreads exposure across different economies, currencies and sectors, so one country's struggles have less effect on the whole portfolio.

## Extra risks

- **Currency risk**: returns to a US investor change with exchange rates. A falling foreign currency reduces returns in dollars, and a rising one adds to them.
- **Political and regulatory risk**: changes in government policy, capital controls or weaker investor protections, especially in some emerging markets.
- **Costs**: international funds often have slightly higher expense ratios than US funds.

## How much?

There is no single right answer. Some investors hold international stocks roughly in proportion to their share of global market value; others hold less, citing currency risk and higher costs. Many target-date funds include a meaningful international allocation.
