# Growth vs Value Stocks

Stocks are often grouped into two investing styles, growth and value, based on how the market prices them relative to their fundamentals.

## Growth stocks

Growth companies are expected to increase revenue and earnings faster than average. Investors pay high prices relative to current earnings, so growth stocks tend to have high price-to-earnings and price-to-book ratios. They often pay small or no dividends and reinvest profits to expand. Because much of their value depends on future earnings, they can be very sensitive to changes in expectations and interest rates.

## Value stocks

Value stocks trade at low prices relative to earnings, book value or cash flow. They are often mature, slower-growing companies, or firms facing problems that have pushed the price down. Many pay dividends. Value investors believe some of these stocks are priced too pessimistically.

## Performance over time

Academic research found that value stocks outperformed growth stocks over long historical periods, which some attribute to extra risk and others to investor behavior. But leadership switches for long stretches: growth stocks dominated for much of the 2010s. No one reliably predicts which style will lead next.

## Using style funds

Index providers publish growth and value versions of major indexes, and many funds track them. A total market fund holds both styles in market proportions. Tilting toward one style is a deliberate bet that adds its own risk and can lag the overall market for years.
