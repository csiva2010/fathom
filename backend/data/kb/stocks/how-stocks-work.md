# How Stocks Work

A stock, also called a share or equity, is a small piece of ownership in a company. When you own a share you own a fraction of the company's assets and future profits, and you may get a vote on certain company decisions.

## Why companies issue stock

Companies sell shares to raise money for growth without taking on debt. The first sale to the public is called an initial public offering, or IPO. After that, shares trade between investors on exchanges such as the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq. When you buy a share on an exchange, you usually buy it from another investor, not from the company.

## How stock investors can earn money

There are two main ways. The first is price appreciation: if the company grows and investors become willing to pay more for its shares, the price rises. The second is dividends: some companies share part of their profits with shareholders as regular cash payments. Many fast-growing companies pay no dividend and reinvest their profits instead.

## What moves stock prices

In the short term, prices move with supply and demand, which respond to news, earnings reports, interest rates and investor mood. Over the long term, prices tend to follow a company's ability to grow its earnings. This is why a single day's move says little about a company's long-term value.

## Common ways stocks are described

Market capitalization, or market cap, is the share price multiplied by the number of shares outstanding. It is used to group companies into large-cap, mid-cap and small-cap. Stocks are also grouped by sector, such as technology, health care or energy, and by style, such as growth or value.

## Risks to understand

A single company can lose most of its value or go bankrupt, and shareholders are paid last if that happens. Holding just a few stocks concentrates that risk. Many beginners get stock-market exposure through diversified funds instead of picking individual companies.
