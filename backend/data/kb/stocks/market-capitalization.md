# Market Capitalization: Large, Mid and Small Caps

Market capitalization, or market cap, is the total market value of a company's shares. It is calculated as share price multiplied by the number of shares outstanding, and it is the usual way to describe a company's size.

## Size categories

Exact cut-offs vary between index providers, but rough ranges are:

- **Large cap**: about $10 billion and up. Well-established companies, often household names.
- **Mid cap**: roughly $2 billion to $10 billion.
- **Small cap**: roughly $250 million to $2 billion.
- **Micro cap**: below that.

## Why size matters to investors

Smaller companies have historically been more volatile and more sensitive to economic conditions, and their shares can be less liquid. They may also have more room to grow. Large companies tend to be more diversified and stable, though they can still fall sharply in downturns.

## Market cap versus share price

A high share price does not mean a company is big. A $500 stock with 10 million shares (market cap $5 billion) is smaller than a $50 stock with 1 billion shares ($50 billion). Stock splits change the share price but not the market cap.

## Market-cap weighting in indexes

Many indexes, including the S&P 500, weight companies by market cap, so the largest firms have the biggest influence on the index's moves. When a handful of very large companies dominate, a "broad" index fund can be more concentrated than it appears. Equal-weight indexes are an alternative that gives each company the same weight.
