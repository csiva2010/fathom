# Dividends Explained

A dividend is a payment a company makes to its shareholders, usually in cash, out of its profits. Not all companies pay dividends; many growing companies reinvest profits instead.

## How dividends are paid

The company's board decides whether to pay a dividend and how much. US companies that pay dividends often do so quarterly. A few dates matter:

- **Declaration date**: the board announces the dividend.
- **Ex-dividend date**: you must own the shares before this date to receive the payment. On the ex-dividend date, the share price typically drops by roughly the dividend amount.
- **Payment date**: the cash arrives in your account.

## Dividend yield

Dividend yield = annual dividends per share / share price. A $100 stock paying $3 a year yields 3%. A very high yield can be a warning sign: it may reflect a falling share price because investors fear the dividend will be cut.

## Reinvesting dividends

Many brokers offer dividend reinvestment, which automatically buys more shares with each payment. Over long periods, reinvested dividends have made up a significant part of total stock market returns.

## Taxes

In taxable accounts, dividends are taxed in the year received, even if reinvested. "Qualified" dividends are taxed at the lower long-term capital gains rates if holding-period rules are met; others are taxed as ordinary income. Inside retirement accounts such as IRAs and 401(k)s, dividends are not taxed when paid.

## Dividends are not guaranteed

Companies can reduce or stop dividends, especially in hard times. A dividend is a share of profits, not a promised interest payment like a bond's coupon.
