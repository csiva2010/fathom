# Risk and Return: Why They Travel Together

In investing, risk and return are linked. Investments that have offered higher long-run returns have also come with bigger ups and downs, and a real chance of losing money, especially over short periods.

## What "risk" means here

Risk is the chance that an investment's actual results differ from what you expected, including the possibility of losing some or all of your money. For most investors the practical question is how much the value could fall, and for how long, before it recovers.

## The general pattern

- **Cash and savings accounts** are very stable but have historically barely kept up with inflation.
- **Bonds** usually move less than stocks and pay interest, but they carry interest rate and credit risk.
- **Stocks** have historically delivered the highest long-run returns of the three but can drop sharply, sometimes 30% to 50% in a severe bear market.

The extra return investors expect for accepting more uncertainty is often called a risk premium. It is an expectation, not a promise: riskier assets can underperform for many years.

## Time horizon changes the picture

Short-term swings matter most if you need the money soon. Money for a goal one or two years away is usually kept in steadier assets, because there is little time to recover from a fall. Money for goals decades away can ride out more volatility, which is why long-term savers often hold more stocks.

## Risk you can reduce

Some risk comes from owning a single company or sector and can be reduced through diversification. Market-wide risk, the tendency of most stocks to fall together in a downturn, cannot be diversified away, and is the part of risk investors are generally compensated for bearing.
