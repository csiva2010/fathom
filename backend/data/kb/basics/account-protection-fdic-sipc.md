# Account Protection: FDIC and SIPC

FDIC insurance and SIPC protection are often confused. Both protect customers when a financial institution fails, but they cover different things, and neither protects against investment losses.

## FDIC: bank deposits

The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation insures deposits at member banks, such as checking accounts, savings accounts, money market deposit accounts and certificates of deposit. The standard coverage is $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category. If an insured bank fails, the FDIC makes depositors whole up to that limit. Credit unions have similar coverage through the NCUA.

FDIC insurance does not cover stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs, annuities or crypto assets, even if bought through a bank.

## SIPC: brokerage customers

The Securities Investor Protection Corporation protects customers of member broker-dealers if the firm fails and customer cash or securities are missing. Coverage is up to $500,000 per customer, including up to $250,000 for cash. In most broker failures, customer assets are simply transferred to another firm.

SIPC does not protect against market losses. If your shares fall in value because the market drops, that is investment risk, not something SIPC covers. It also does not cover bad investment advice or fraud by the issuer of a security.

## Practical takeaways

- Money you cannot afford to see fall in value belongs in insured deposits or similar stable holdings.
- Some brokerages "sweep" uninvested cash into partner banks, where it may be FDIC insured; check how your broker handles cash.
- You can verify a bank's FDIC status with the FDIC's BankFind tool and a broker's registration with FINRA BrokerCheck.
