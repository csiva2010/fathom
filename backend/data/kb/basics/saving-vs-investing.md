# Saving vs Investing: What's the Difference?

Saving and investing both mean setting money aside for later, but they serve different purposes and carry different risks. Most people need both.

## Saving: safety and access

Saving usually means keeping money in bank accounts, credit union accounts or similar places where it is easy to reach and the balance does not fall. Bank deposits are generally insured by the FDIC up to legal limits. The trade-off is a modest return that may not keep pace with inflation over long periods.

Saving fits money you may need within a few years: an emergency fund, a planned purchase, or a down payment in the near future.

## Investing: growth with risk

Investing means buying assets such as stocks, bonds or funds with the aim of growing your money over time. Investments can earn more than savings accounts, but their value goes up and down, and you can lose money. Investments are not FDIC insured.

Investing fits money you will not need for many years, such as retirement savings, because a long horizon gives time to recover from downturns.

## A common order of steps

Educational resources often describe a sequence like this:

1. Build a small cash cushion for emergencies.
2. Pay down high-interest debt, since its interest cost is often higher than likely investment returns.
3. Capture any employer retirement match.
4. Grow the emergency fund toward several months of expenses.
5. Invest regularly for long-term goals.

Everyone's situation differs, but matching each pot of money to its time horizon is the core idea.
