# The Rule of 72

The Rule of 72 is a quick mental shortcut for estimating how long it takes money to double at a given annual growth rate. Divide 72 by the yearly rate, and the answer is roughly the number of years to double.

## How it works

- At 6% a year: 72 / 6 = about 12 years to double.
- At 8% a year: 72 / 8 = about 9 years.
- At 3% a year: 72 / 3 = about 24 years.

The rule is an approximation that works best for rates between roughly 4% and 12%. For exact answers, a compound interest calculator is better, but the shortcut is handy for building intuition.

## Using it for inflation and fees

The same idea shows how fast costs grow. If inflation averages 3%, prices roughly double in 24 years, meaning a fixed income would buy about half as much. It also shows the drag of fees: a portfolio earning 7% doubles in about 10 years, but at 6% after a 1% fee it takes about 12 years.

## Using it for debt

Debt compounds too. A credit card balance at 24% interest that is never paid down would roughly double in three years. This is one reason high-interest debt is often treated as an urgent priority.

## A reminder about returns

Investment returns are not steady. Stocks might rise 20% one year and fall 15% the next. The Rule of 72 assumes a constant average rate, so treat its answer as an illustration of compounding rather than a forecast.
