# The Time Value of Money

The time value of money is the idea that a dollar today is worth more than a dollar in the future, because today's dollar can be invested and earn a return in the meantime. It underlies loans, bond prices, retirement projections and company valuations.

## Future value

Future value answers "what will this amount grow to?" If you invest $1,000 at 6% a year, it grows to $1,060 after one year and keeps compounding after that. The longer the time and the higher the rate, the larger the future value.

## Present value

Present value runs the math in reverse: "what is a future amount worth today?" If you will receive $1,000 in five years, its present value is less than $1,000, because you could invest a smaller sum today and reach $1,000 by then. Converting future amounts into today's dollars is called discounting, and the rate used is the discount rate.

## Where you see it

- **Bonds**: a bond's price is the present value of its future interest payments and principal. When market interest rates rise, those fixed payments are discounted more heavily, so the price falls.
- **Loans and mortgages**: payments are set so their present value equals the amount borrowed.
- **Goal planning**: projections estimate the future value of savings and contributions, while "how much do I need to save each month" is a present-value style question.
- **Valuing companies**: analysts estimate future cash flows and discount them to a present value.

## Inflation is part of the story

A dollar in the future also buys less if prices rise. Projections often show both nominal values and inflation-adjusted, or real, values so you can see what a future balance might buy in today's terms.
