# Money Market Funds

A money market fund is a type of mutual fund that invests in high-quality, short-term debt such as Treasury bills, government agency debt and commercial paper. It aims to keep a stable value, usually $1 per share, while paying interest that tracks short-term rates.

## How they are used

Money market funds are popular for cash waiting to be invested, for the cash portion of a portfolio, and as the sweep account in many brokerage accounts. Their yields rise and fall with short-term interest rates, so they pay more when the Federal Reserve's rates are high.

## Types

- **Government** money market funds hold almost entirely government securities and repurchase agreements backed by them.
- **Prime** funds also hold corporate short-term debt and pay slightly more, with slightly more risk.
- **Tax-exempt** funds hold short-term municipal debt; their income is generally free from federal tax.

## Money market fund versus money market account

They sound alike but differ. A money market deposit account is a bank account insured by the FDIC. A money market fund is an investment and is not FDIC insured. Funds are tightly regulated and losses have been very rare, but in extreme stress a fund could fall below $1 a share, as one did in 2008.

## Strengths and limits

Money market funds offer daily liquidity and low volatility, which makes them useful for short-term money. Over long periods, though, their returns have tended to roughly match inflation, so they are not a substitute for long-term investments.
