# Target-Date Funds

A target-date fund is an all-in-one fund built around the year you expect to retire, such as a "2060 fund." It holds a mix of stocks and bonds and gradually becomes more conservative as that year approaches.

## The glide path

The fund's planned shift from stocks toward bonds and cash over time is called its glide path. A fund decades from its target date might hold about 90% stocks; near the target date, it might hold closer to half stocks. Some glide paths keep adjusting for years after the target date ("through" funds), while others stop at the target date ("to" funds).

## Why people use them

- **Simplicity**: one fund provides a diversified portfolio and automatic rebalancing.
- **Defaults**: many 401(k) plans use target-date funds as their default investment.
- **Behavior**: an automatic approach can help people avoid emotional trading.

## Things to check

- **Fees**: costs vary widely, from very low index-based funds to more expensive actively managed series.
- **Glide path**: funds with the same target year can hold quite different amounts of stocks.
- **Overlap**: holding a target-date fund plus other funds can undo its intended allocation. They are designed to be used on their own.

## Not guaranteed

A target-date fund can lose money, including near or after its target date. The date is a planning assumption, not a promise of a particular balance or income.
