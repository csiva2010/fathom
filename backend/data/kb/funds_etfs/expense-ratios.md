# Expense Ratios

The expense ratio is the annual fee a mutual fund or ETF charges, expressed as a percentage of your investment. It covers management, administration and other operating costs.

## How it is charged

You never receive a bill. The fund deducts its expenses from its assets day by day, and the returns it reports are already net of them. With a 0.20% expense ratio, a $10,000 investment costs about $20 a year.

## Typical ranges

- Broad index funds and ETFs: often under 0.10%.
- Actively managed stock funds: commonly 0.5% to 1% or more.
- Specialized or alternative funds: can be higher still.

## Why it matters

Costs are one of the most reliable predictors of fund performance: every dollar paid in fees is a dollar that does not compound for you. The difference between 0.05% and 1.00% may look small, but over 30 years it can reduce an ending balance by a significant amount.

## Gross and net expense ratios

Some funds list a gross ratio and a lower net ratio after temporary fee waivers. The waivers can expire, so the gross figure shows the potential cost.

## Other costs not in the ratio

The expense ratio does not include trading commissions, sales loads, bid-ask spreads for ETFs, or the fund's internal trading costs. A fund's prospectus describes all of these. This assistant's portfolio analysis shows a weighted expense ratio across your holdings so you can see your overall cost.
