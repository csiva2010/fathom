# How to Read a Fund Prospectus

Every mutual fund and ETF must provide a prospectus describing the fund. Most funds also publish a shorter summary prospectus and a fact sheet. You do not need to read every page, but a few sections answer most questions.

## Investment objective and strategy

This section explains what the fund is trying to do, such as "track the S&P 500 Index" or "seek current income," and how: what it buys, how concentrated it is, and whether it is actively managed.

## Fee table

Near the front, a standard table lists shareholder fees (such as sales loads and redemption fees) and annual operating expenses, including the total expense ratio. An example shows what you would pay on a $10,000 investment over 1, 3, 5 and 10 years, which makes comparing funds easier.

## Principal risks

A list of the main ways you could lose money, such as market risk, interest rate risk, credit risk, foreign investment risk or concentration risk. Matching these to what you expected is a good sanity check.

## Past performance

A bar chart of annual returns and a table comparing the fund with a benchmark index over 1, 5 and 10 years. Past performance does not predict future results, but it shows how volatile the fund has been and how it compares with its index after fees.

## Other useful details

- **Portfolio turnover**: how much the fund trades each year; high turnover can mean higher hidden costs and taxes.
- **Management**: who runs the fund and for how long.
- **Purchase and sale rules**: minimums, and how and when you can buy or sell shares.

Prospectuses are available on fund company websites and the SEC's EDGAR database.
