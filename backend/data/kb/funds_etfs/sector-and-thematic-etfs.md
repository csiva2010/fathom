# Sector and Thematic ETFs

Sector ETFs hold companies in one part of the economy, such as technology, health care or energy. Thematic ETFs focus on a trend, such as clean energy, robotics or cybersecurity. Both offer targeted exposure, and both come with concentration risk.

## The 11 sectors

The widely used Global Industry Classification Standard divides the stock market into 11 sectors: information technology, health care, financials, consumer discretionary, communication services, industrials, consumer staples, energy, utilities, real estate and materials. Each has a family of sector ETFs, and this assistant's market overview uses sector ETFs to show which parts of the market are up or down on a given day.

## Why sectors behave differently

Sectors respond differently to the economy. Consumer staples and utilities tend to be steadier because people buy groceries and pay electric bills in any economy. Technology and consumer discretionary can swing more with growth expectations and interest rates. Energy follows oil and gas prices.

## Risks of narrow funds

- **Concentration**: a single sector or theme can fall far more than the overall market.
- **Overlap**: a broad index fund already owns every sector, so adding sector funds tilts the portfolio rather than diversifying it.
- **Late entry**: thematic funds often launch after a theme has become popular and prices have already risen. Research has found many thematic funds underperform after launch.
- **Costs**: thematic ETFs often have higher expense ratios than broad funds.

## How they are commonly used

Many investors who use them keep sector and thematic funds as a small part of a portfolio built around broadly diversified funds.
