# Leveraged and Inverse ETFs

Leveraged ETFs aim to deliver a multiple, such as 2x or 3x, of an index's daily return. Inverse ETFs aim to deliver the opposite of the daily return, and leveraged inverse funds combine the two. Regulators have repeatedly warned that these products are complex and generally unsuitable for buy-and-hold investors.

## The daily reset

These funds target their multiple for a single day. They rebalance every day to maintain it, so over longer periods their returns can differ greatly from the multiple of the index's return.

## A simple example

Suppose an index rises 10% one day and falls 9.09% the next, ending where it started. A 2x leveraged fund would gain 20%, then fall 18.18%: $100 becomes $120, then about $98.18. The index is flat, but the fund lost almost 2%. In choppy, volatile markets this effect, often called volatility decay, keeps eroding value.

## Other risks

- **Magnified losses**: a 3x fund can lose a third of its value in a day when the index falls 10%.
- **Costs**: expense ratios and internal financing costs are typically higher than for ordinary index funds.
- **Path dependence**: results depend on the sequence of daily returns, not just the start and end points.

## Who uses them

They are mainly used by active traders for short-term positions or hedging, monitored closely. FINRA guidance notes that, for most investors with long time horizons, they are not appropriate as long-term holdings.
