# Active vs Passive Funds

Funds fall into two broad camps. Active funds have managers who try to beat the market by choosing securities or timing trades. Passive funds, mainly index funds, simply aim to match a market index.

## The case for passive

Before costs, investors as a group earn the market's return, because together they own the market. After costs, the average actively managed dollar must therefore trail the average passively managed dollar, since active funds charge more. Studies such as S&P's SPIVA reports have consistently found that a large majority of active US stock funds underperform their benchmarks over 10 to 15 years, and past winners rarely keep winning.

## The case for active

Active managers can avoid parts of the market they consider overvalued, may do better in less efficient markets, and some do outperform for long periods. Active management is also common in areas like municipal bonds, where indexing is harder.

## Hidden differences

- **Costs**: active funds usually charge higher expense ratios and trade more, adding hidden costs.
- **Taxes**: higher turnover tends to produce more taxable capital gains distributions.
- **Consistency**: an active fund's style can drift, and manager changes can alter its strategy.

## Choosing

Many investors use low-cost index funds as their core holdings. Those who choose active funds often look for low costs, long manager tenure and a clear, consistent strategy, while accepting that underperformance is possible for years at a time.
