# Dollar-Cost Averaging

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals, such as every payday, regardless of market prices.

## How it works

Because the dollar amount is fixed, you automatically buy more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high. Over time, this can result in a lower average cost per share than the average price over the same period.

Most people already dollar-cost average without thinking about it: contributing to a 401(k) from every paycheck is DCA.

## Behavioral benefits

- Removes the temptation to time the market.
- Builds a consistent saving habit.
- Makes downturns feel less threatening, since lower prices mean your contributions buy more shares.
- Reduces the regret of investing a large amount right before a drop.

## DCA versus lump sum

If you already have a large sum, such as an inheritance or bonus, the choice is between investing it all at once or spreading it out. Studies have found that lump-sum investing has historically produced higher returns about two-thirds of the time, because markets have risen more often than they have fallen and money waiting in cash misses out. Spreading the investment over several months mainly reduces the risk of regret if markets fall soon after.

## Choosing

Many people choose lump sum for the better expected outcome, or DCA over a defined period if the emotional comfort helps them invest at all. Either is generally better than leaving long-term money in cash indefinitely while waiting for a "right time."
