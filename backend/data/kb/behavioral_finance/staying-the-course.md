# Staying the Course in a Downturn

Market downturns are a normal part of investing, yet they are when many investors make their most costly decisions. Planning for them ahead of time makes it easier to stay on track.

## Why downturns are hard

Falling balances trigger fear and loss aversion. News coverage amplifies worst-case scenarios. Selling feels like taking control, but it often turns temporary paper losses into permanent ones and risks missing the recovery, which historically has frequently started while news was still bad.

## Preparing before the storm

- **Emergency fund**: cash for job loss or surprise expenses means you are not forced to sell investments at low prices.
- **Right-sized allocation**: hold a stock and bond mix you can live with in a 30% or larger stock decline.
- **Written plan**: an investment policy statement describing your goals, allocation and what you will do in a downturn.
- **Automation**: automatic contributions and rebalancing keep you on course without repeated decisions.

## During the downturn

- Remember your time horizon. Money for goals decades away has time to recover.
- Keep contributing if you can; you are buying at lower prices.
- Rebalance according to your plan, which may mean buying stocks when they are down.
- Check your accounts less often.
- In taxable accounts, consider whether tax-loss harvesting applies.

## When change is appropriate

If a downturn reveals that your allocation was more aggressive than you can tolerate, adjust it thoughtfully, ideally after markets calm, rather than in a moment of panic.
