# Herd Behavior and FOMO

Herd behavior is the tendency to follow what everyone else is doing. In investing, it often appears as fear of missing out (FOMO): rushing into whatever has recently soared because others seem to be getting rich.

## How it fuels bubbles

When prices rise, stories of big gains spread, more people buy, and prices rise further. Social media can accelerate the cycle. Eventually prices detach from fundamentals, and when sentiment turns, the herd heads for the exit together. Historical examples include the dot-com bubble around 2000, the housing bubble of the mid-2000s, and episodes in meme stocks and crypto assets.

## Warning signs

- An investment is popular mainly because its price has gone up.
- People with no background in it are suddenly recommending it.
- Claims that "this time is different" or that old valuation rules no longer apply.
- Pressure to act immediately.
- Promises of guaranteed or extraordinary returns, which are also red flags for fraud.

## Herding works in reverse too

During crashes, the herd sells in panic, often near market bottoms.

## Ways people resist the herd

- Set your plan before you hear the hype, and judge new ideas against it.
- Limit speculative bets to a small portion of your portfolio that you could afford to lose.
- Ask what you would need to believe for the price to make sense.
- Wait a few days before acting on excitement.

FINRA and the SEC publish investor alerts on hype-driven and social-media-promoted investments.
