# Confirmation Bias

Confirmation bias is the tendency to seek out, favor and remember information that supports what we already believe, while discounting information that contradicts it.

## How it affects investors

- After buying a stock, reading mostly bullish articles and dismissing warning signs.
- Following online communities or commentators who share your views, creating an echo chamber.
- Interpreting ambiguous news as support for your existing position.
- Remembering past calls that worked and forgetting those that did not.

Combined with overconfidence, confirmation bias can lead to concentrated positions held long after the original reasoning stopped making sense.

## Why it matters

Good decisions depend on seeing the full picture, including the risks. Markets incorporate both positive and negative views; ignoring one side means missing information that other investors are already acting on.

## Ways to counter it

- **Make the opposing case**: before investing, write down the strongest reasons it could go wrong.
- **Seek out disagreement**: read analyses that reach the opposite conclusion.
- **Set criteria in advance**: decide what evidence would make you change your mind, before you need it.
- **Use checklists**: a consistent process reduces the influence of selective attention.
- **Diversify**: a broadly diversified portfolio limits the damage when any single conviction turns out wrong.

Recognizing that everyone is prone to confirmation bias is the first step; building habits that expose you to contrary evidence is the practical defense.
