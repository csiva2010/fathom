# Overconfidence Bias

Overconfidence bias is the tendency to overestimate our own knowledge, skill or ability to predict the future. In investing, it can lead to costly decisions.

## How it shows up

- **Excessive trading**: confident investors trade more, believing they can pick winners. Academic studies of brokerage accounts found that the most active traders earned noticeably lower returns than the least active, largely because of trading costs and poor timing.
- **Under-diversification**: confidence in a few picks leads to concentrated portfolios.
- **Illusion of control**: believing that research or activity gives more control over outcomes than it really does.
- **Attributing success to skill**: crediting gains to ability and blaming losses on bad luck, which reinforces confidence after lucky streaks.

## Why markets are humbling

Markets reflect the combined information and opinions of millions of participants, including professionals with large research budgets. Consistently outguessing them is very hard, which is why most professional fund managers trail their benchmark indexes over long periods.

## Ways to keep it in check

- Keep a written record of investment decisions and the reasons, then review results honestly.
- Compare your results against a simple index benchmark.
- Hold core savings in diversified funds; limit stock-picking to a small portion.
- Seek out views that disagree with yours before acting.

Some confidence is necessary to invest at all. The problem is certainty about things that are inherently uncertain.
