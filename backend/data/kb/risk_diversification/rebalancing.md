# Rebalancing a Portfolio

Rebalancing means bringing a portfolio back to its target asset allocation after market movements have pushed it off course.

## Why portfolios drift

Suppose you aim for 60% stocks and 40% bonds. After a strong year for stocks, the mix might become 70/30. The portfolio is now riskier than you intended. After a crash, it might become 50/50, more conservative than planned.

## How rebalancing works

You sell some of what has grown beyond its target and buy what has fallen below it, or simply direct new contributions toward the underweight assets. Rebalancing is mainly about controlling risk, though it also has the effect of trimming assets that have risen and adding to ones that have fallen.

## When to rebalance

- **Calendar approach**: once a year, for example.
- **Threshold approach**: whenever an asset class drifts more than a set amount, such as 5 percentage points, from its target.

Research suggests that rebalancing very frequently adds costs without much benefit, while never rebalancing lets risk creep up.

## Keeping costs and taxes down

- Rebalance inside tax-advantaged accounts where selling has no tax consequences.
- Use new contributions, dividends and withdrawals to nudge the mix.
- In taxable accounts, be aware that selling winners can realize capital gains.

## Automatic options

Target-date funds, balanced funds and many robo-advisers rebalance automatically, which also removes the emotional difficulty of buying an asset that has just fallen.
