# Risk Tolerance vs Risk Capacity

Two different ideas are often lumped together as "risk": how much risk you are comfortable with, and how much risk you can actually afford. Good planning considers both.

## Risk tolerance

Risk tolerance is your emotional and psychological willingness to accept ups and downs. Someone with high risk tolerance can watch their portfolio fall 30% and stay the course; someone with low tolerance may lose sleep over a 10% drop. Questionnaires, like the one in this assistant's onboarding, ask how you would react to losses and what trade-offs you prefer, then summarize the result as a profile such as conservative, moderate or aggressive.

## Risk capacity

Risk capacity is your financial ability to absorb losses without derailing your goals. It depends on factors such as:

- How soon you need the money.
- Job and income stability.
- Size of your emergency fund.
- Other assets, pensions or guaranteed income.
- Debts and dependents.

A 25-year-old with a stable job saving for retirement has high capacity even if they feel nervous. A retiree drawing on savings for living expenses may have lower capacity even if they feel bold.

## When the two disagree

If tolerance is higher than capacity, taking the risk you feel comfortable with could endanger essential goals. If capacity is higher than tolerance, a very aggressive portfolio may lead to panic selling at the worst time. Many planners suggest the lower of the two as a guide.

## Revisit over time

Both change with age, income, family and market experience. Reassessing after big life events keeps allocations aligned.
