# Concentration Risk and Single-Stock Positions

Concentration risk is the danger of having too much of your money in one investment, sector or type of asset. If that one thing does badly, your whole portfolio suffers.

## How concentration happens

- Receiving company stock through an employer plan, stock options or restricted stock units.
- A single stock that grows to dominate a portfolio after a big run-up.
- Buying several funds that all hold the same large companies or the same sector.
- Emotional attachment to a familiar company.

## Why it matters

Even large, successful companies can fall sharply and stay down for years, and some fail entirely. Research has found that a large share of individual stocks underperform the overall market over their lifetimes, while a small number of big winners drive most market gains. Holding a broad index captures those winners without needing to pick them.

Owning a lot of your employer's stock adds a second layer: if the company struggles, your job and your savings could be hit at the same time.

## How concentration is often measured

Simple checks include the largest single holding's share of the portfolio and the share in the top five holdings or largest sector. Some educational guidelines flag any single stock above about 10% of a portfolio. This assistant's diversification score is concentration-based and explains which holdings drive it.

## Ways people reduce it

Common approaches include selling gradually over time, directing new money elsewhere, donating appreciated shares, or using exchange funds. Taxes on large gains often shape the approach, so it is worth discussing a big concentrated position with a tax professional.
