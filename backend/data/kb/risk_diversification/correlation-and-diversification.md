# Correlation: Why Diversification Works

Correlation measures how closely two investments move together. It is the reason diversification can reduce risk without necessarily giving up return.

## The correlation scale

Correlation ranges from -1 to +1:

- **+1**: the two always move in the same direction by proportional amounts.
- **0**: no consistent relationship.
- **-1**: they always move in opposite directions.

## Why low correlation helps

If you combine two volatile investments that do not move in lockstep, losses in one are often partly offset by gains or smaller losses in the other. The combined portfolio swings less than either holding alone. This insight is a cornerstone of modern portfolio theory, introduced by Harry Markowitz.

## Typical relationships

- Stocks within the same industry are usually highly correlated.
- US and international stocks are positively but not perfectly correlated.
- High-quality bonds have often had low or negative correlation with stocks, especially in sharp stock market sell-offs, though both fell together in 2022 when interest rates rose rapidly.
- Cash has very low correlation with stocks.

## Correlations change

Correlations are not fixed. In financial crises, many risky assets tend to fall together, so diversification can feel weaker exactly when it is most wanted. That is one reason high-quality bonds and cash, rather than other risky assets, are often used as stabilizers.

## Practical takeaway

Owning many investments only diversifies if they behave differently. Ten technology stocks are less diversified than ten companies across different sectors and countries.
