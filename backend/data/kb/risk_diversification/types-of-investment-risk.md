# Types of Investment Risk

"Risk" in investing covers several different ways things can go wrong. Knowing the main types helps explain why different investments behave differently and why diversification works.

## Market risk

The chance that an entire market falls, pulling most investments down with it, as in a recession or financial crisis. It cannot be eliminated by diversifying within the same market.

## Company-specific risk

The chance that one company stumbles because of poor management, a failed product, a lawsuit or fraud. Owning many companies greatly reduces this risk.

## Interest rate risk

When interest rates rise, existing bond prices fall. Long-term bonds are the most affected. Rate changes also influence stock valuations and real estate.

## Inflation risk

The chance that returns do not keep pace with rising prices, shrinking purchasing power. It is the main long-term risk of holding too much cash.

## Credit (default) risk

The chance a bond issuer cannot pay interest or principal. Higher-yield bonds carry more of it.

## Liquidity risk

The chance you cannot sell an investment quickly at a fair price. Some bonds, private investments and small stocks can be hard to sell in a hurry.

## Currency risk

For international investments, changes in exchange rates can add to or subtract from returns.

## Longevity and sequence risk

In retirement, the risk of outliving savings, and of poor returns arriving just as withdrawals begin.

## Putting it together

No investment is free of every risk; cash avoids market risk but faces inflation risk, and stocks fight inflation but bring market risk. Balancing these, matched to goals and time horizon, is the job of asset allocation.
