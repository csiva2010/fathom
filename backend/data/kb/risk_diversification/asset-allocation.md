# Asset Allocation

Asset allocation is how you divide your investments among asset classes such as stocks, bonds and cash. It is one of the most important investing decisions, because it largely determines how much your portfolio can grow and how much it can fall.

## Why the mix matters

Different asset classes respond differently to economic conditions. Stocks offer more growth potential but more volatility; bonds usually move less and provide income; cash is stable but grows slowly. Combining them lets you choose a level of risk that suits you. Studies of diversified portfolios have found that the stock/bond mix explains most of the variation in their returns over time.

## What shapes the right mix

- **Time horizon**: the longer until you need the money, the more short-term volatility you can usually tolerate.
- **Risk tolerance**: how much decline you can live with emotionally without selling in a panic.
- **Risk capacity**: how much loss your finances can absorb, given income stability, other savings and obligations.
- **Goals**: different goals can have different allocations.

## Example mixes

Educational illustrations often describe conservative portfolios with mostly bonds and cash, moderate portfolios split roughly between stocks and bonds, and aggressive portfolios with mostly stocks. These are generic examples, not personal recommendations; this assistant's goal planner shows such examples labeled as education.

## Allocation changes over time

Many people shift gradually toward bonds as a goal approaches, which is exactly what target-date funds automate. Markets also move allocations away from their targets, which is why rebalancing exists.
