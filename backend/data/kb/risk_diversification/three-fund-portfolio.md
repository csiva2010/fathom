# The Three-Fund Portfolio

The three-fund portfolio is a simple, widely discussed way to build a diversified portfolio using just three low-cost index funds. It is popular in the Bogleheads community, which follows the investing philosophy of Vanguard founder John Bogle.

## The three building blocks

1. **A total US stock market index fund**: thousands of US companies of all sizes.
2. **A total international stock index fund**: companies in developed and emerging markets outside the US.
3. **A total US bond market index fund**: a broad mix of investment-grade government and corporate bonds.

Together, these hold most of the world's publicly traded stocks and a large slice of the US investment-grade bond market.

## Why people like it

- **Diversification**: broad exposure across thousands of securities.
- **Low cost**: total market index funds have very low expense ratios.
- **Simplicity**: easy to understand, monitor and rebalance.
- **Tax efficiency**: broad stock index funds distribute few capital gains.

## Choosing the proportions

The approach does not prescribe a fixed mix. The split between stocks and bonds reflects time horizon and risk tolerance, and the split between US and international stocks is a personal judgment. A target-date fund is essentially a pre-packaged version that adjusts the mix automatically.

## Variations

Some add a fourth fund, such as international bonds or TIPS. Others use a single balanced fund. The core idea, owning the whole market cheaply and sticking with it, matters more than the exact funds.
