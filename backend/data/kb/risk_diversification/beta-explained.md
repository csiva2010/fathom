# Beta: Measuring Market Sensitivity

Beta measures how much a stock or fund has tended to move relative to the overall market, usually the S&P 500. It is a measure of market sensitivity.

## Reading beta

- **Beta of 1**: has moved in line with the market on average.
- **Beta above 1**: has moved more than the market. A beta of 1.3 means a 10% market move has historically come with about a 13% move in the same direction.
- **Beta below 1**: has moved less than the market. Utilities and consumer staples often have low betas.
- **Negative beta**: tends to move opposite the market, which is rare.

## Portfolio beta

A portfolio's beta is roughly the weighted average of its holdings' betas. This assistant's portfolio analysis uses weighted beta as one of its risk measures, alongside volatility and drawdown.

## Limitations

- Beta is backward-looking and changes over time as companies and markets change.
- It depends on the period and benchmark used to calculate it.
- It measures only market-related movement. A stock can have a low beta yet be very risky because of company-specific events.
- Beta says nothing about expected return on its own.

## Systematic versus specific risk

Beta captures systematic risk, the part of an investment's swings tied to the market as a whole, which diversification cannot remove. The remaining specific risk can be reduced by holding many investments. This distinction is the foundation of much of modern portfolio theory.
