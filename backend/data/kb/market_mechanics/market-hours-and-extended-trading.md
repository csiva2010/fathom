# Market Hours and Extended-Hours Trading

Major US stock exchanges hold their regular trading session from 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time, Monday through Friday, except on market holidays.

## Extended-hours sessions

Many brokers also offer pre-market trading before the open and after-hours trading after the close. Some now offer overnight trading in selected securities. Extended-hours activity often picks up when companies release earnings or other news outside regular hours.

## Risks of trading outside regular hours

- **Lower liquidity**: fewer participants means it can be harder to complete a trade.
- **Wider spreads**: the gap between bid and ask is often larger.
- **More volatility**: prices can swing more on thin trading.
- **Price disconnects**: an extended-hours price may differ sharply from the next day's opening price.
- **Order restrictions**: many brokers accept only limit orders in extended hours.

## Market holidays and early closes

US markets close for holidays including New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Presidents' Day, Good Friday, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas. Some days, such as the day after Thanksgiving, have early closes. Exchanges publish their holiday calendars.

## What this means for quotes

Outside the regular session, a quoted "last price" may come from thin after-hours trading or simply be the previous close. This assistant labels each number with its source and time so you can tell whether data is live or from an earlier session.
