# How Stock Exchanges Work

A stock exchange is a marketplace where buyers and sellers trade shares. In the US, the best-known exchanges are the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and Nasdaq, but trading also happens on many other venues.

## Primary and secondary markets

When a company first sells shares to the public, in an initial public offering (IPO), it raises money in the primary market. After that, investors trade those shares with each other in the secondary market. Most everyday trading is secondary: when you buy a stock, you buy it from another investor, not from the company.

## How a trade happens

1. You place an order with your broker.
2. The broker routes it to an exchange, an alternative trading system or a market maker.
3. The order is matched with an opposite order at an agreed price.
4. The trade settles: shares and cash change hands, typically one business day later in the US (T+1).

## Market makers and liquidity

Market makers are firms that continuously post prices at which they will buy (bid) and sell (ask), making it easier for others to trade at any moment. They earn a small profit from the spread between those prices. Their activity is a major source of liquidity.

## Regulation

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) oversees US securities markets, and FINRA regulates broker-dealers. Rules require brokers to seek the best reasonably available execution for customer orders.

## How prices are set

Prices move as buyers and sellers react to news, earnings, economic data and changing expectations. A stock price at any moment is simply the most recent price at which a buyer and seller agreed to trade.
