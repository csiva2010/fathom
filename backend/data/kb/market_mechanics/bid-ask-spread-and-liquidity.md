# The Bid-Ask Spread and Liquidity

Every tradable security has two prices at any moment: the bid, the highest price buyers are offering, and the ask, the lowest price sellers will accept. The difference is the bid-ask spread.

## A hidden trading cost

If a stock's bid is $50.00 and its ask is $50.04, buying with a market order costs about $50.04, and selling immediately would get about $50.00. The four-cent difference is a cost of trading, even with zero commissions. For frequent traders, spreads add up.

## What determines the spread

- **Trading volume**: heavily traded stocks and ETFs often have spreads of a penny.
- **Volatility**: spreads widen when prices are moving quickly or uncertainty is high.
- **Price level and size**: thinly traded small companies, some bonds and niche ETFs can have much wider spreads.
- **Time of day**: spreads tend to be wider near the open and close and outside regular hours.

## Liquidity

Liquidity is how easily you can buy or sell without moving the price much. Highly liquid securities have tight spreads and deep order books, meaning many shares available near the current price. Illiquid securities may require accepting a worse price to trade quickly.

## Practical tips often given

- Use limit orders for less liquid securities so you control the price.
- Be cautious trading right at the open, near the close or after hours.
- Compare spreads when choosing between similar ETFs, especially for frequent trading.

For long-term investors who trade rarely, spreads are a small cost, but they still matter for large or frequent trades.
